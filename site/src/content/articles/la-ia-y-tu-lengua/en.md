## Why your language is not in the translator

An AI model learns languages the way it learns everything: by reading. For English there
are trillions of words available; for Spanish, a great many; for Nahuatl or Yucatec Maya,
a tiny fraction — and for hundreds of languages of the Americas, almost nothing digital.
Without texts and recordings, there is nothing to learn from. The result is a vicious
circle: because the language is not in the technology, it is used less digitally; because
it is used less, there is less data; because there is less data, it never reaches the
technology.

## What is lost if we do nothing

It is not just convenience. When a language does not exist for machines, its speakers are
left out of services already taken for granted: dictating a message, asking an assistant
for information, subtitling a video, completing a digital government form. And there is a
worse risk: that AI "learns" the language from the outside, from data taken without
permission, and returns an impoverished or wrong version that ends up being taught as if
it were the real one.

## Proof that it can be done

The Māori people of New Zealand proved it. Their community radio network, Te Hiku Media,
had spent decades recording elders speaking te reo Māori. When AI arrived, the community
organized recording marathons: thousands of people donated hours of voice reading
sentences. With that they trained their own speech recognizer — today it transcribes Māori
better than the systems of the big companies. And they set a condition that made history:
the data is neither sold nor given away; it is stewarded under their own license, in which
**benefit flows back to the source**. African languages have their network (Masakhane),
the languages of the Americas have allied researchers (AmericasNLP) — the path exists.

## What it would take here

Three things, in this order. **Voice**: recordings of speakers of all ages — read
sentences, conversation, stories. **Text**: parallel translations (the same sentence in
Spanish and in the language), which is exactly what a machine translator needs in order to
learn. **Governance**: clear agreements, written in plain language, about who keeps the
data, what it is used for and how the benefit returns to the community — before recording
the first word, not after.

That is the CIUDADAN-IA project: this outlet informs in your language, and the next step
is built with you. If you speak Nahuatl, Maya or another original language, your voice is
literally what is missing.
