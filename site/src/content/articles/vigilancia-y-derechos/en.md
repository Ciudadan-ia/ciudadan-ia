## What is being installed, exactly

Three technologies are spreading through the region's cities, often purchased without
public debate. **Facial recognition**: cameras that compare every passing face against
databases — sometimes of suspects, sometimes of all citizens. **Social media monitoring**:
systems that track what is posted about certain topics and who posts it. **"Predictive"
policing**: programs that say where to patrol based on past crime statistics.

## Where the problem lies

First, **the errors are not evenly distributed**. Facial recognition systems are trained
mostly on faces from other latitudes: they fail more on brown skin, on Indigenous
features, on women. A "false positive" is not a statistic — it is a person detained for a
crime they did not commit, and such cases are already documented.

Second, **the crosshairs point crooked**. In several countries of the region, these tools
have been used not against crime but against journalists, opposition figures and land
defenders. For a community protesting a megaproject, "security" can mean being watched.

Third, **prediction inherits injustice**. If police always patrolled poor neighborhoods
more, the data says more crime happens there, so the system sends more patrols there.
Prejudice enters dressed as mathematics.

## Not every use is equal

Neither panic nor hype: AI also finds missing persons, detects child abuse imagery and
helps in disasters. The difference between legitimate use and abuse is not set by the
technology — it is set by rules: is there a law authorizing it? A court order? A public
registry of which systems the government uses? A path to appeal if it affects you? Where
those answers are "no," the tool rules over the right.

## What you can demand

At minimum: **transparency** (which systems your government bought, from whom, with what
results), **limits** (banning mass facial recognition in public spaces, as several
congresses are already debating), **independent auditing** (someone external measuring the
errors and against whom they fall) and **remedies** (the power to challenge a decision
made by a machine). Surveillance installs itself in silence; rights are defended out loud.
