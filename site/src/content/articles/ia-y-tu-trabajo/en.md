## The question is badly framed

"Will AI take my job?" sounds like yes or no, but jobs are not blocks: they are bundles of
tasks. A secretary doesn't "do secretarying": they schedule, draft, file, receive people,
resolve conflicts, hold the office's memory. AI doesn't compete against the whole bundle —
it competes against individual tasks.

That explains why catastrophic predictions ("half of all jobs will disappear") have been
failing for decades, and also why total calm is naive: even if your job doesn't disappear,
its content is going to change.

## Which tasks are in the line of fire

The ones AI absorbs fastest share three traits: they involve **text or data**, they are
**repetitive**, and they have a **verifiable result**. Transcribing meetings, drafting
documents, sorting requests, translating standard paperwork, answering frequent questions,
generating routine reports. If part of your week consists of producing predictable text,
that part already has competition.

## Which tasks resist — and why

What resists are tasks with a body, with context and with trust. With a **body**:
repairing a water pump, caring for a patient, harvesting on uneven terrain — robotics
moves far slower than software. With **context**: deciding what to do when the manual
doesn't apply, reading an organization's internal politics, negotiating with a supplier
you've known for years. With **trust**: people want certain things signed, said or decided
by an accountable human — a diagnosis, a ruling, an apology.

## What you can do starting today

First, take honest inventory: what percentage of your week is repetitive text? That is
your real exposure. Second, use the tools before they are imposed on you: someone who
masters AI for their trade is worth more, not less — the machine does the draft, you bring
the judgment. Third, invest in the irreplaceable: relationships, reputation, manual craft,
local knowledge. And fourth, demand that the transition be fair: the productivity AI
generates doesn't distribute itself — it gets distributed where workers are organized and
informed.
